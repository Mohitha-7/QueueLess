    Designing a line you don’t have to stand in

    01Project overview

    QueueLess is a mobile app concept that lets college students book a time slot at a campus service counter, receive a digital token and follow their place in line from anywhere. It replaces standing in a physical queue with a short, predictable visit.

    RoleUI/UX design and front-end prototyping
    PlatformMobile, 393 × 852 px frame
    Scope7 screens, 4 campus services
    StatusConcept with working prototype

    The prototype is a front-end simulation. It is not connected to a college database or a live queue system.

    02Problem statement

    At many colleges, services such as fee payment, certificates and administration run on walk-in queues. Students do not know how long they will wait, whether they carry the right documents, or whether leaving the line costs them their place. A short errand can take a large part of a break between lectures.

    How might we…

    …tell students exactly when to arrive and what to bring, so a counter visit fits between classes and does not depend on standing in line?

    This framing is based on common experience of campus queues, not on collected data.

    03Project goals

    Student goals

    • Book a service slot in under a minute.
    • Know the wait before leaving the classroom.
    • Arrive with the right documents.
    • See and cancel bookings without help.

    Design goals

    • One clear action per screen.
    • A token that is readable at a glance.
    • Errors that explain how to recover.
    • Consistent data across every screen.

    Targets such as “under a minute” are design aims to check in testing, not measured results.

    04Target audience

    Primary: college students aged 18–25, mostly undergraduates, who move between lectures with limited free time and use their phone for most tasks.

    Not in scope for this concept: counter staff and administrators. A staff-side tool would be a separate design effort.

    10Design decisions and reasoning

    Navy with a single teal accent

    Navy reads as institutional and trustworthy. Teal is reserved for progress and success, so it keeps its meaning. Teal text uses a deeper variant to pass contrast.

    The token is the hero

    At 56–64px and high contrast, the token can be read from arm’s length at a counter, and it is the one thing a student must remember.

    Full slots explain themselves

    Unavailable times use a hatch, a dashed border, strike-through and a “Full” label. Tapping one gives a reason and two open alternatives instead of failing silently.

    Documents before booking

    The checklist sits on the details screen, before commitment, so students can decide whether they are ready.

    Wait time everywhere

    Estimated wait appears on cards, details, token and queue. It is the number students ask first.

    Plain destructive confirmations

    Leaving a queue or cancelling opens a sheet that states the consequence, with the safe option first.

    Three tabs only

    Home, My Bookings and Profile cover everything. The booking flow hides the tab bar to keep attention on one task.

    One booking record

    Home, token, queue and My Bookings all read the same booking, so token number, time and status never disagree.

    11Accessibility considerations

    Contrast ratios below are calculated from the palette. Pairs under 4.5:1 are used only for fills, borders and icons.

    Navy on white13.9:1 · AAA
    Primary text14.6:1 · AAA
    Secondary text4.8:1 · AA
    Teal Deep on tint4.7:1 · AA
    Error Deep on tint5.2:1 · AA
    Teal on white3.3:1 · graphics only
    • Not colour alone: slot state, status pills and errors combine colour with text, pattern or icons.
    • Touch targets: buttons, slots and tabs are 48px or larger; primary actions are 56px.
    • Text size: body 15px, captions 12px minimum. A Larger text setting scales the app text by 14%.
    • Reduce motion: a setting switches off animation and respects the system preference on this page.
    • Semantics: real buttons, switches with state, a progress bar with values, alerts for errors, labelled dialogs and Escape to close sheets.
    • Not yet done: screen-reader testing, keyboard-only walkthrough with real users and a full WCAG audit.

    12Challenges and design solutions

    ChallengeSolution
    Showing a full slot without a dead endVisible “Full” treatment, tap feedback with shake, a message naming the slot and the next open times.
    Making a queue feel live without a backendScripted ticks every 3.5 seconds while the screen is open, labelled as a simulation in the prototype notes and in the app.
    Keeping data consistent across screensA single booking object drives Home, Token, Live Queue and My Bookings.
    Avoiding double bookingA second booking for today is blocked while a token is active, with a message explaining how to proceed.
    Fitting dense content into 393pxSegmented Upcoming/History, two-column service grid, summary footer in the slot step.
    Teal accent failing text contrastTeal limited to graphics. A derived deep teal carries small text.

    13Usability testing plan

    Proposed plan · no sessions have been run

    Method

    Moderated, think-aloud sessions of 20–25 minutes on the clickable prototype, run on a phone where possible.

    Participants

    5–8 undergraduates from different years and courses, including at least one who uses larger system text.

    Tasks

    1. Find out how long the Fee Payment wait is and what to bring.
    2. Book the 09:30 AM slot (expected to be full) and recover.
    3. Confirm a booking for 10:30 AM and tell the moderator what your token is.
    4. Check how many people are ahead of you.
    5. Leave the queue, then find the booking afterwards.

    Measures and proposed success criteria

    MeasureHowProposed target
    Task completionObserved, unaidedAt least 80% on tasks 1–3
    Time to bookStopwatch, tasks 2–3Under 60 seconds after the error
    Errors and hesitationsModerator notesRecovery from full slot without help
    Perceived easeSingle Ease Question per task, SUS at endBaseline to be set after round one
    ComprehensionAsk participants to explain token and positionToken and position explained correctly

    Next steps

    1. Write a short screener and consent note, then recruit participants.
    2. Pilot the script with one person and fix prototype blockers.
    3. Run sessions and record issues by severity.
    4. Replace the persona and pain-point assumptions with what the sessions show.
    5. Prioritise fixes, update the prototype and run a second round.
